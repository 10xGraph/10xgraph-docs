**An audit log is an append-only record of who did what, to which resource, when, and with what result.** It answers questions after the fact: who read this thread, who deleted that file, who started this job.

## Why does it matter for agents?

An agent server acts on behalf of users and can change data in many places. Metrics tell you how the system performs; an audit log tells you who acted. Auditors for frameworks such as SOC 2 and ISO 27001 ask for this kind of evidence.

## What should it contain?

Keep the record small: the actor, the action, the resource id, the time and the outcome. Leave message content out. A log full of prompts and replies becomes another store of sensitive data that needs its own protection.

## How does 10xGraph do it?

In `10xgraph-api`, the `audit` key in `10xgraph.json` turns on audit events with `enabled`, `sinks` (`log`, `publisher` or `module:Class`) and `include_reads`. Events go to the `tenxgraph_api.audit` logger, and `LOG_FORMAT=json` makes them machine-readable. 10xGraph emits the events and does not store them; retention and access control are yours. See the [audit log guide](/docs/server/audit-log).
